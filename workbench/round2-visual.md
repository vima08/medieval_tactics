## Biggest gap: battlefield hierarchy

The large map now fills more of the central view than in the earlier capture, but it still reads first as a uniform green tile lattice. Ten small figures, four signal flags, elevation numbers, grass marks, ridges, bridges, and river all carry similar visual weight ([large battle](round2-shots/06-big-map.png)). At a glance, the flags are easy to mistake for decoration and the classes rely on tiny weapons or the roster labels for recognition. The preview introduces a useful line and target outline, but these effects are thin against the many diagonal tile edges ([attack forecast](round2-shots/03b-attack-preview.png)). This is the biggest gap against polished indie tactics: the game has meaningful board information, yet the first visual read does not clearly rank units and objectives above terrain detail.

**Concrete fix to test:** establish a stronger board hierarchy in one visual pass. Reduce base tile grid and grass contrast; give each unit a slightly larger team-colored base and a class-specific silhouette or shield mark; make each capturable signal fire a distinct bright beacon with a visible ownership ring. Keep the current preview geometry, but thicken its route/target strokes so it remains legible over elevation seams. Compare before/after screenshots at 1440 × 900 and 1280 × 720. A five-second first look should let a new viewer point to all four objectives, distinguish team and class for each figure without the side roster, and trace the previewed action without reading the forecast panel. Check the same states with selection and preview off to ensure the hierarchy works before interaction.

## Other observations

- The menu has strong type and a consistent palette, but no depiction of a battle, unit, or terrain ([menu](round2-shots/01-menu.png)). A small piece of game art would connect the title to the actual play screen.
- The roster's top Start button is now visible and resolves the previously reported hidden primary action. The six class cards still differentiate mainly through small glyphs and text; the left area below them is largely empty ([roster](round2-shots/05-roster.png)).
- The tutorial's selected state and forecast convey move cost and attack damage. The forecast text is very small at this resolution, though the line and highlighted target help ([move](round2-shots/03a-move-preview.png), [attack](round2-shots/03b-attack-preview.png)).
- Pause and result dialogs are legible and consistent with the interface. The result presentation is sparse and loses the battlefield almost entirely under its dark scrim; it gives little visual payoff for the outcome ([blue result preview](round2-shots/08-result-blue-preview.png)).
